## 🔹 **1. Confusion Matrix**

A **confusion matrix** is a tabular representation of a classifier’s performance, showing the counts of correct and incorrect predictions for each class.

For binary classification:

|                     | **Predicted Positive**  | **Predicted Negative**  |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Actual Positive** | **TP (True Positive)**  | **FN (False Negative)** |
| **Actual Negative** | **FP (False Positive)** | **TN (True Negative)**  |

---

## 🔹 **2. Precision**

**Definition:**
Precision measures the proportion of correctly predicted positive instances out of all instances predicted as positive.

[
\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}
]

**Interpretation:**
High precision indicates that the classifier makes **few false positive** errors.
It evaluates the **exactness** or **quality** of positive predictions.

**Example:** Used in spam detection to ensure legitimate emails aren’t incorrectly labeled as spam.

---

## 🔹 **3. Recall (Sensitivity / True Positive Rate)**

**Definition:**
Recall measures the proportion of correctly predicted positive instances out of all actual positive instances.

[
\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}
]

**Interpretation:**
High recall indicates that the classifier correctly identifies **most of the positive samples**, minimizing false negatives.
It evaluates the **completeness** of the classifier.

**Example:** Used in medical diagnosis where missing a positive case (false negative) is costly.

---

## 🔹 **4. F1-Score**

**Definition:**
The **F1-Score** is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, providing a single metric that balances both.

[
\text{F1-Score} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}
]

**Interpretation:**
The F1-Score is useful when the class distribution is **imbalanced**, or when both false positives and false negatives are important.

---

## 🔹 **5. Accuracy**

**Definition:**
Accuracy measures the overall proportion of correctly classified instances.

[
\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
]

**Limitation:**
Accuracy can be **misleading for imbalanced datasets**, as it may be high even when the model performs poorly on the minority class.

---

## 🔹 **6. Relationships & Trade-offs**

* **Precision vs Recall Trade-off:**
  Increasing precision usually decreases recall, and vice versa.
  Adjusting the **decision threshold** (e.g., in logistic regression) controls this trade-off.

* **F1-Score:**
  Optimal when you need to find a balance between precision and recall.

---

## 🔹 **7. Summary Table**

| Metric        | Formula                   | Focus               | Sensitive to    |
| ------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------- |
| **Precision** | TP / (TP + FP)            | Exactness           | False Positives |
| **Recall**    | TP / (TP + FN)            | Completeness        | False Negatives |
| **F1-Score**  | 2 × (P×R)/(P+R)           | Balance             | Both FP & FN    |
| **Accuracy**  | (TP + TN) / (TP+TN+FP+FN) | Overall correctness | Class imbalance |
